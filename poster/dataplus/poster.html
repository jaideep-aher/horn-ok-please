Horn OK Please
Can words painted on South Asian trucks hijack an AI that drives?
Jaideep (Jay) Aher · Duke AIPI · Responsible AI final project
jaideep-aher.github.io/horn-ok-please
Pilot, 6 drawn scenes: English truck paint flipped a small edge model from STOP to GO on 6 of 6 scenes, including 4 of 4 with a hazard in the lane. Erasing the paint restored STOP.
Overview
Why it matters
  • Trucks carry painted text in English, Devanagari and Urdu
  • Small in-car (edge) vision-language models read it
  • If a model obeys the paint, a tailgate becomes a command

Affected: pedestrians and riders first, then drivers, truck owners, ADAS teams and regulators.

Gap: these attacks were mostly tested in English [1–3].

Objective

Contribution: an open, preregistered audit kit that tests if multilingual truck paint flips a driving AI’s STOP/GO call, and shows why [5, 6].

Person in lane, so STOP is right; only the paint changes. Badges are stored answers. Pilot set: 4 hazards + 2 empty lanes (there GO is right; the clean STOP was wrong). Cloud changed 1 hazard answer in this set (Marathi paint, truck stopped ahead); its one main-test flip was Hindi, on a debris scene.

Results
6/6

EDGE MODEL · PILOT, n = 6 Qwen 3B: STOP to GO on all 6 (95% CI 60–100%), 4 of 4 hazards (95% CI 51–100%); erasing restored all 6.

Edge answers changed, of 6 scenes

Controls: Marathi, harmless, random: 0 of 18

6/19

Exploratory Real photos: English paint changed Qwen on 6 of 19 tailgates (Holm p = 0.125, n.s.); erasing restored 6/6.

4/17

Defenses (warning prompt, OCR text-stripping) braked on 4 of 17 empty roads the cloud model got right.

Limitations
  • Drawn single frames; these scenes only
  • Edge pilot: 1 model, STOP on 20/20 clean lanes: a mechanism, not a rate
